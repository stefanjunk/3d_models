# Evidence, sources, and interpretation limits

Retrieved 2026-08-13; exposed-infill sources rechecked 2026-08-17. Recheck current slicer/material documentation before relying on version-specific behavior.

## Process and DfAM guidance

- NIST, [Design Rules for Additive Manufacturing](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=921515) — formal DfAM context and the need to make process constraints explicit. Treat broad AM rules as a framework, not process-specific dimensions.
- Stratasys, [FDM for End-Use Parts](https://www.stratasys.com/siteassets/3dlc/high-school/tag_fdm_enduseparts_en_1015_web.pdf) — variable-density regions, support-aware rib alteration, inserts, and build-oriented design. Its machine/software values are not universal desktop-FFF settings.
- Prusa, [Modeling with 3D printing in mind](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135) — official process-aware modeling guidance.

## Walls, infill, local reinforcement, and flow

- Prusa, [Layers and perimeters](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/layers-and-perimeters_1748) — perimeters materially influence strength and exact multi-path wall thickness depends on extrusion spacing/layer height.
- Prusa, [Modifiers](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modifiers_1767) — local settings can change infill, layers/perimeters, extrusion width, and related controls; official examples include locally removing top/bottom solid layers. Boundary behavior must still be checked in the exact project.
- Prusa, [Infill patterns](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/infill-patterns_177130) — documents pattern-specific topology and toolpaths, including alternating rectilinear directions, same-layer grid crossings and buildup, and gyroid behavior. Use these as slicer/profile-specific starting evidence, not universal mechanical claims.
- Prusa, [Arachne perimeter generator](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/arachne-perimeter-generator_352769) — variable-width walls can reduce gap fill and short extrusions; always inspect the actual paths.
- Prusa, [Different nozzle types](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/different-nozzle-types_2193) and [Maximum volumetric speed](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/max-volumetric-speed_127176) — nozzle/detail trade-offs and the flow ceiling that can erase expected speed gains.
- Mazlan et al., [Effects of Infill Density, Wall Perimeter and Layer Height on the Tensile Properties of 3D Printed PLA Specimens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9867140/) — controlled evidence that walls, infill, and layer height interact; do not extrapolate its specimen/process values directly to another material or geometry.
- Nikiema et al., [Study of walls' influence on the mechanical properties of 3D printed specimens](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10462826/) — experimental evidence that wall configuration matters, reinforcing the need for process-matched testing.
- Agrawal et al., [Combined effect of infill pattern, density, and layer thickness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10238722/) — multi-parameter interactions; no single infill recommendation is universally optimal.
- Dezaki et al., [Influence of Infill Patterns Generated by CAD and FDM 3D Printer on Surface Roughness and Tensile Strength](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/11/16/7272) — evidence that modeled CAD cellular patterns and slicer-generated infill are not interchangeable.

## Topology and optimization

- Yan et al., [Printing Ready Topology Optimization for Material Extrusion Additive Manufacturing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10133987/) — process-aware topology optimization must account for printability instead of producing an abstract minimum-volume shape.
- Syrlybayev et al., [Optimisation of Strength Properties of FDM Printed Parts—A Critical Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8157110/) — review of coupled process/material/orientation effects and evidence limits.

Use topology optimization only after defining loads, constraints, orientation, minimum printable members, and a validation plan. A visually organic result is not evidence of stiffness, fatigue life, or support-free manufacture.

## Watertight and fluid systems

- Prusa, [Watertight prints](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/watertight-prints_112324) — even walls, multiple perimeters, seams, temperature, and the warning that watertight appearance is not automatic.
- UltiMaker, [How to 3D print waterproof parts](https://ultimaker.com/learn/how-to-3d-print-waterproof-parts/) — distinguishes material, process, and post-processing contributions; not a pressure certification.
- Zhang et al., [Additive manufacturing of products with functional fluid channels: A review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addma.2020.101490) — research context for channel manufacturability and application-specific verification.

Never convert these sources into a generic pressure rating or potable/food claim.

## Mesh complexity and exchange

- Prusa, [Simplify mesh](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/simplify-mesh_238941) — dense meshes can slow slicing and produce many short toolpaths.
- ManifoldCAD, [`simplify(tolerance)`](https://manifoldcad.org/docs/jsuser/classes/Manifold.html#simplify) — documents a physical maximum surface movement for its simplifier.
- CGAL, [Triangulated Surface Mesh Simplification](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Surface_mesh_simplification/index.html) — boundary/volume-aware edge-collapse strategies and optional envelope constraints.
- OrcaSlicer, [STL transformation and Simplify model](https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/wiki/prepare_stl_transformation) — high-polygon meshes increase slicing load; aggressive simplification can lose detail or create print artifacts.
- 3MF Consortium, [Core specification, meshes](https://github.com/3MFConsortium/spec_core/blob/master/3MF%20Core%20Specification.md#41-meshes) — manifold-edge, orientation, normal, non-degeneracy, and component-reuse requirements.

Mesh simplification primarily improves data handling and may shorten paths when dense facets become small segments. It does not inherently reduce deposited material or print time; measure the final toolpaths.

## Evidence policy

- Treat official slicer documentation as behavior for the documented version/profile family, not a mechanical guarantee.
- Treat peer-reviewed coupons as evidence of factor interactions, not transferable allowables.
- Treat formulas/calculators as candidate generators.
- Require exact-process slicing and physical coupons where geometry, anisotropy, sealing, fatigue, or fit decides acceptance.
- Record uncertainty and negative results; they are reusable design evidence.
