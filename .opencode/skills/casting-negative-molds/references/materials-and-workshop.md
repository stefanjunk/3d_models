# Materials and workshop practice

Use the selected material manufacturer's current technical and safety documents. The values below describe workflow, not a substitute mix recipe.

## Printed tooling materials

### PLA

- Easy to print and dimensionally stable at normal room temperature.
- Can soften under heat; do not use hot drying or exothermic processes without a measured temperature margin.
- Layer lines and pinholes need finishing when the tool must be watertight.
- Often suitable for masters, cases, prototypes, and sacrificial shells.

### PETG

- Better water and impact resistance than typical PLA but can string and produce flexible thin walls.
- Sanding can smear; coating adhesion must be tested.
- Useful for reusable cases and direct gypsum molds when properly designed.

### ABS/ASA

- Can be vapor-smoothed under controlled professional practice, but large parts warp and emissions require proper controls.
- Solvent treatment changes dimensions and surface chemistry.

### Filled/abrasive filaments

Avoid carbon, glass, metal, wood, glow, or other filled filaments on precision casting faces unless their benefit is proven. Fillers increase abrasion, roughness, porosity, and possible contamination while complicating sealing.

### Photopolymer resin

- High detail and smoothness are useful for masters/inserts.
- Fully wash and cure to the resin manufacturer's process.
- Verify water, plaster, silicone, and release compatibility.
- Treat uncured resin and wash liquids as hazardous; prevent contamination of casting materials and drains.

## Surface finishing of printed masters and cases

The working goal is a controlled surface, not indiscriminate smoothing.

1. Fill voids and layer-line valleys on the printed **master/case**.
2. Sand without rounding critical relief.
3. Apply a compatible sealer only where the printed tool must be nonporous or release plaster/silicone.
4. Measure critical dimensions again after every coating.
5. Apply the chosen release system as specified for the exact tool/casting pair.
6. Make a small chemistry and detail-transfer coupon.

Do not seal the slip-contact face of an absorbent plaster working mold.

## Pottery-plaster working molds

Pottery plaster is formulated and processed to create controlled porosity and strength. Follow the product datasheet for:

- water-to-powder ratio by mass;
- water temperature;
- soaking/slaking time;
- mixing time and intensity;
- vacuum/deairing or vibration practice;
- set and demold time;
- drying procedure and maximum safe drying temperature.

Small ratio changes affect strength, pore structure, absorption, wear, and casting behavior. Weigh water and powder; do not mix by eye.

### Mixing workflow

- Use clean tools and water; contamination can accelerate or retard set.
- Add plaster to water uniformly rather than dumping a compact mass.
- Allow the specified soak, then mix with minimal air entrainment.
- Pour a thin stream into the lowest area and let the level rise.
- Brush or vibrate the first face coat carefully around fine texture.
- Keep a reservoir available as the level settles.
- Never add water or dry powder after the mix begins setting.

### Air control

Bubbles collect on downward-facing or horizontal cavity features. Use:

- a low pour point;
- a thin first face coat brushed into detail;
- controlled tapping/vibration;
- vacuum mixing where appropriate;
- vents in the case for displaced air.

Do not over-vibrate and separate the mix.

### Drying and storage

An absorbent ceramic working mold must dry sufficiently between casts. Provide:

- airflow on all faces;
- racks that do not distort or chip sections;
- no sealed plastic storage while damp;
- labeled matched sets;
- protected casting faces;
- moisture or mass tracking for repeatable production.

Excessive heat can damage plaster. Follow the exact manufacturer's temperature limit.

## Direct plaster casting into printed molds

Before a full pour:

1. Verify the mold is watertight.
2. Verify coating/release does not inhibit or stain the plaster.
3. Measure the mix exotherm in a representative test volume.
4. Confirm the print remains stiff at that temperature.
5. Confirm clamps and seams tolerate hydrostatic pressure.
6. Add vents and a top-up reservoir.
7. Define a safe demold time from actual tests.

Use mechanical mixing, dust extraction/respiratory protection, eye protection, and gloves as required by the product SDS. Never use setting plaster for direct body casting because heat and expansion can cause severe injury.

## Release-system logic

Choose the release for the interface:

- plastic master ↔ plaster;
- plastic case ↔ plaster mold section;
- plaster parting surface ↔ new plaster section;
- plastic mold ↔ final gypsum cast;
- printed master ↔ silicone;
- silicone ↔ plaster/resin.

A product that works at one interface may fail at another. Apply the thinnest complete film consistent with the manufacturer's instructions; excess release can pool in engraving and erase detail.

## Workshop geometry

A digitally valid mold can be unusable in a real workshop. Include:

- maximum safe section mass for the operator;
- handholds and lifting points;
- bench clearance and assembly orientation;
- a way to support fragile greenware during opening;
- drip trays and a drain stand;
- cleaning tools that reach every channel;
- storage cradles that protect casting faces;
- labels readable after coating and repeated use.

## Waste and contamination

- Keep plaster out of drains; let residue settle and dispose of it according to local rules.
- Keep clay/slip reclaim streams separated from plaster contamination.
- Keep resin, solvent, coating, and release waste out of ceramic reclaim unless specifically qualified.
- Do not use food-preparation tools or sinks for mold chemicals.
- Document every coating/release on tools intended for food-contact ceramic production.
