## 1. Separate Digital and Physical Releases

| Topic | Digital model/file | Printed physical product |
|---|---|---|
| Main customer promise | File conforms to description, format, scale, compatibility, license | Product conforms to description and is safe |
| Product liability | Defective manufacturing file can cause harm; EU recast PLD expressly includes digital manufacturing files | Ordinary defective-product exposure plus sector law |
| Process variability | State approved production envelope and limits | Seller controls qualified process and batch |
| Traceability | Release ID, file hash, download/customer version | Release ID plus batch/lot, material, machine, inspection |
| Customer terms | Digital content, updates/support, withdrawal/refund, file license | Sale terms, warranty, returns, warnings |
| Recall/correction | Withdraw file, notify downloaders, issue fixed version | Stop sale, quarantine/recall stock, notify supply chain/users |
| Insurance | Technology/E&O and product liability may both matter | Product/public liability; recall cover where appropriate |

Selling both requires both workstreams. A disclaimer such as “print at your own risk” does not eliminate mandatory consumer or product liability.

## 2. Safety Classification Gate

Record the exact intended use and foreseeable misuse. Then answer:

- Is it used by or near children?
- Does it contact food, drinking water, skin, body tissue, medicine, or cosmetics?
- Does it carry, suspend, retain, brake, climb, guard, seal, pressurize, heat, or electrically insulate?
- Does failure risk injury, fire, shock, choking, entrapment, falling objects, poisoning, contamination, or property damage?
- Is it part of machinery, a vehicle, aircraft/drone, construction work, medical system, PPE, weapon, electrical/radio equipment, gas/pressure system, or safety system?
- Is it represented as a replacement part or compatible accessory?
- Does it include magnets, batteries, chemicals, sharp points, small parts, cords, or high temperatures?
- Does it process personal/biometric data or include software/connectivity?

If yes or uncertain, BLOCK commercial release until the product category and applicable rules/standards are documented.

## 3. EU Physical-Product Baseline

### General Product Safety Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2023/988 applies from 13 December 2024 to consumer products placed or made available in the EU where product-specific Union law does not fully cover the risk.

For a seller that is the manufacturer of a printed product, plan for:

- design/manufacture consistent with the general safety requirement;
- internal risk analysis and technical documentation;
- a type, batch, serial, or other identifier;
- manufacturer name/trade name and postal and electronic address;
- clear instructions and safety information in languages set by the Member State, unless safely usable without them;
- conformity checks for series production and changes;
- complaint register, investigation, corrective action, withdrawals/recalls, and authority cooperation;
- retention of technical documentation for 10 years after market placement;
- an EU-established responsible economic operator where required;
- required information in distance/online offers.

GPSR does not authorize a CE mark.

### Online Offer Information

Plan to display clearly and visibly:

- manufacturer name/trade name and postal/electronic address;
- EU responsible person/economic operator contact where manufacturer is outside the EU;
- product identification, including picture/type/identifier;
- warnings/safety information required by law.

Confirm the marketplace form actually displays the data to buyers; a hidden seller profile may be insufficient.

### Responsible Operator

If the manufacturer is outside the EU, determine the responsible economic operator under applicable rules: importer, authorized representative with the right mandate, fulfillment service provider in defined cases, or sector-specific actor. Do not list a party without a signed mandate and operational ability to hold documentation and cooperate.

## 4. EU Digital Manufacturing File Liability

Directive (EU) 2024/2853 recasts product liability. It expressly treats a “digital manufacturing file” containing functional information necessary to produce a tangible item by automated machinery or tools, such as drills, lathes, mills, and 3D printers, as a product.

Member States must transpose the Directive by 9 December 2026. It applies to products placed on the market or put into service after that date. Verify national implementing law and transitional rules.

Commercial implications:

- a defective CAD/STL/3MF that causes a resulting product to be unsafe can create strict product-liability exposure;
- file updates, learning/adaptation, cybersecurity, expected use, instructions, and product presentation can matter to defect analysis;
- calling a file “digital only” does not avoid product analysis;
- the free/open-source exclusion is limited to software developed or supplied outside a commercial activity; a paid file or commercially supplied open file should not rely on it;
- retain file version/customer records so a dangerous version can be corrected;
- maintain product liability and technology/E&O insurance appropriate to digital manufacturing files.

Treat this framework as an immediate design control even for releases before national transposition, because files may remain on sale after the operative date.

## 5. CE and Sector Conformity

CE marking is mandatory only for products covered by EU harmonisation legislation requiring it. It is prohibited to affix CE where no such rule requires it. CE is not EU approval and not a general quality label.

Potential regimes include:

- toys;
- electrical equipment and electromagnetic compatibility;
- radio equipment;
- machinery and safety components;
- personal protective equipment;
- medical devices and in vitro diagnostics;
- construction products;
- pressure equipment;
- measuring instruments;
- recreational craft;
- pyrotechnics/explosives;
- hazardous substances/environmental requirements that interact with product design.

For an applicable regime:

1. define manufacturer, product, intended purpose, variant family, and market;
2. identify exact legislation and essential requirements;
3. identify current harmonised standards or alternative technical specifications;
4. select conformity-assessment route and determine whether a notified body is required;
5. design and verify against requirements;
6. compile technical documentation;
7. issue/sign the EU declaration of conformity/performance as applicable;
8. apply required mark, identifiers, actor contacts, instructions and languages;
9. maintain production conformity and post-market controls;
10. retain records for the statutory period.

A certification on a bought-in part does not certify the final product.

### Product-Specific Stop Categories

Obtain specialized review and testing for:

- toys, child-care products, small parts, magnets;
- food-contact, potable-water, medical, dental, skin-contact;
- electrical, battery, radio, EMC, fire/heat;
- PPE, guards, lifting/load-bearing, climbing, vehicle/aviation;
- pressure, gas, fluid containment;
- building/construction and structural parts;
- replacement safety components;
- weapons or weapon accessories;
- products marketed with health, rescue, protective, or compliance claims.

Additive-manufacturing anisotropy, porosity, aging, UV/moisture/chemical exposure, creep, sterilization, and process variance can invalidate assumptions from bulk material datasheets.

## 6. UK, US, and Other Markets

### Great Britain and Northern Ireland

Classify under UK product-specific rules and current marking arrangements. GB and Northern Ireland can differ. Verify UKCA/CE acceptance, responsible actor, conformity assessment, product safety, importer details, language, and online-sale duties on the planned launch date.

### United States

There is no single universal CE-equivalent. Identify federal and state rules by product:

- Consumer Product Safety Commission statutes/standards and reporting;
- children’s product testing/certification and tracking labels;
- FDA jurisdiction for food-contact/medical/cosmetic-related uses;
- FCC for radio/electronic emissions;
- NHTSA/FAA/ATF/other agencies for relevant products;
- state chemical warnings, privacy, biometrics, warranty, and product-liability law;
- local electrical/building/fire codes and recognized laboratory requirements.

Do not market a hobby test as “FDA approved,” “CPSC certified,” “UL certified,” or equivalent without the actual lawful basis.

### Canada, Australia/New Zealand, Japan, Korea, China, and Others

For each destination:

- identify regulator/product regime and local responsible party;
- check mandatory standards, testing, registration, certification/marks;
- translate labels/instructions;
- evaluate chemicals/materials, electrical/radio, child and consumer safety;
- confirm incident-reporting/recall duties;
- confirm digital-content, privacy, AI disclosure, tax, packaging/EPR, import, and customs rules.

Use local counsel/labs for regulated categories. “Ships worldwide” is not a compliance strategy.

## 7. Risk Assessment and Verification

Use an auditable method such as ISO 12100 principles for machinery-related risk where relevant or another product-appropriate framework. Do not cite a standard without lawful access and edition verification.

At minimum:

1. Define intended use, limits, lifespan, environment, users, and foreseeable misuse.
2. Identify hazards across transport, assembly, use, cleaning, maintenance, foreseeable modification, disposal, and digital fabrication.
3. Estimate severity and probability before controls.
4. Apply hierarchy: inherently safe design, guards/protection, then information/warnings.
5. Define verification method and objective acceptance criteria.
6. Test worst-case geometry, material, process, orientation, aging, load, and scale.
7. Record residual risk and customer instructions.
8. Reassess after any change or incident.

Useful tests may include:

- dimensional and fit gauges;
- tensile/flexural/impact/load/creep/fatigue;
- thermal cycling, heat deflection, flammability;
- UV/moisture/chemical/cleaning aging;
- layer adhesion and orientation sensitivity;
- drop, torque, pull, pinch, sharp-edge/small-parts;
- food-contact migration or biocompatibility only through qualified labs;
- electrical insulation/creepage, EMC, battery abuse only through competent methods;
- misuse and assembly-error testing.

One successful prototype is not series-production validation.

## 8. Production Controls

Lock:

- approved CAD/mesh hash and drawing;
- printer model/firmware;
- slicer/version/profile and post-processor;
- nozzle/resin/process;
- layer/perimeter/infill/orientation/support;
- material specification, approved supplier, lot, storage/drying;
- machine calibration and maintenance;
- post-cure/anneal/cleaning/finishing;
- embedded part supplier/revision;
- inspection and sampling;
- packaging and shipping conditions.

Maintain a change-control review for:

- material color/formulation/supplier;
- printer or firmware;
- slicer algorithm/profile;
- orientation or scale;
- component supplier;
- geometry, watermark, or label;
- standards/law;
- production site.

Trace every sold unit/batch to the release and process. Quarantine nonconforming product.

## 9. Customer Information and Online Offers

State:

- product identity/release/version;
- intended use and exclusions;
- material and relevant process characteristics;
- load/temperature/environment limits supported by tests;
- assembly/installation/maintenance/disposal;
- compatible bought parts and genuine part numbers;
- warnings and residual risks;
- manufacturer/importer/responsible-person contact;
- batch/lot;
- conformity marks/declaration links only when applicable;
- AI-assisted/synthetic-image disclosure as decided;
- support, incident, and recall contact.

For digital files add:

- units, scale, mesh quality, tolerances;
- supported printer/process envelope;
- validation status;
- exact G-code constraints if included;
- prohibited safety-critical uses;
- version update/correction process;
- license and third-party notices;
- how immediate-download consent/withdrawal acknowledgment is captured for EU consumers.

Avoid absolute claims such as “unbreakable,” “food safe,” “child safe,” “medical grade,” “load bearing,” or “certified” unless the complete final product/process and market claim are supported.

## 10. Insurance, Incidents, and Recalls

Before launch:

- disclose digital-file and additive-manufacturing activities accurately to broker/insurer;
- obtain territory/product/category/revenue-appropriate product liability;
- consider technology E&O/cyber, recall, and IP defense/indemnity cover;
- verify exclusions for US/Canada, children, medical, aviation, weapons, exported products, design-only work, and subcontractors;
- align contractual indemnities with available insurance.

Incident process:

1. Preserve evidence and identify release/batch/customers.
2. Stop sale/download and quarantine stock when safety may be involved.
3. Notify safety, legal, insurer, marketplace, and business owners.
4. Determine regulator/consumer reporting and recall duties.
5. Issue controlled correction/recall; never overwrite silently.
6. document root cause, corrective action, revalidation, and closure.

## 11. Export Controls and Sanctions

Digital delivery is an export. Email, cloud access, repository download, remote support, and release of technical data to a foreign person can be controlled.

For every file/product:

- classify under EU dual-use rules and any applicable national military list;
- classify under U.S. EAR/ITAR if U.S.-origin content, persons, tools, or jurisdiction are involved;
- screen destination, customer, beneficial owner, end user, end use, intermediaries, payment, and shipping route;
- evaluate encryption, aerospace, drones, propulsion, navigation, surveillance, weapons, nuclear, advanced materials, and manufacturing technology;
- retain classification, screening, license, and transaction evidence;
- re-screen at shipment/download and on list changes.

BLOCK weapons, defense, aerospace, nuclear, sanctions, or ambiguous dual-use cases for qualified export counsel. Do not use a marketplace’s country block as the only control.

## 12. Tax, Packaging, Privacy, and Business Operations

Add market-specific owners for:

- legal entity/business registration;
- VAT/GST/sales tax, customs value/classification, import duties, marketplace facilitator roles;
- EU VAT OSS/IOSS where applicable;
- packaging registration/EPR, batteries, electrical waste, recycling/material claims;
- terms, privacy notice, cookies/analytics, data processors/transfers;
- accessible storefront and instructions;
- recordkeeping/invoices;
- marketplace and payment-provider policies;
- trademark/domain/SKU governance.

Germany/EU sellers should specifically assess packaging registration and system participation, VAT, product-safety economic-operator data, and sector EPR before first shipment. Use tax/compliance professionals for the actual business footprint.

## 13. Market-Launch Matrix

Do not activate a country until all columns are resolved:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Country/channel | Exact storefront/marketplace and fulfillment route |
| Release | Digital, physical, or both |
| Product category | Legal classification and rationale |
| Regulator/law | Current primary source/effective date |
| Conformity | Assessment, standard, lab/body, registration/mark |
| Economic operator | Manufacturer/importer/representative contact |
| Label/instructions | Identifier, warnings, language, online fields |
| Digital terms | License, conformity, updates, withdrawal/refund |
| AI transparency | Role, modality, disclosure/marking |
| IP | Copyright/design/patent/trademark/privacy result |
| Export/sanctions | Classification and screening |
| Tax/EPR | Registrations and owner |
| Insurance | Territory/category confirmation |
| Evidence | File paths and hashes |
| Approval | Owner, date, PASS/WARN/BLOCK |

No “global” row is permitted. A global strategy is a set of approved country/channel rows.
