# Market research synthesis

## What the local research supports

The most coherent opportunity is not a general 3D-model marketplace. It is a curated system for products that gain value from exact dimensions, modularity, or personalization. The strongest recurring themes in the research files are:

- exact-fit drawer and shelf organization;
- small-space storage that standard products underserve;
- personalized functional decor;
- system-furniture accessories, particularly drawer inserts and non-structural shelf accessories;
- dual digital/printed delivery;
- repeatable parameter and release systems rather than isolated STL files.

The highest-ranked everyday concepts were personalized bookends, shelf-fit bins, entryway panels, utility-rail modules, narrow pullout carts, headboard organizers, and windowsill shelves. The system-furniture analysis ranked ALEX and BROR drawer inserts highly, with an OMAR shelf deck as a possible simple MVP. The mass-customization research also identified vehicle trays, case inserts, board-game organizers, tool-drawer shadow boards, and device mounts.

## What the research does not prove

The files do not contain validated sales volumes, conversion data, customer interviews, tested prices, acquisition costs, return rates, or production margins. Scores and price ranges are prioritization hypotheses. They must not be represented as market facts.

## Portfolio conclusion

Use the research to choose a focused experiment:

- one existing exact-fit design (`DrawerFit`) to test measurement and file-delivery confidence;
- one simple personalized product (`NameForm Bookends`) to test personalization value;
- one small-space storage family (`ShelfFit Mini`) to test fitted dimensions and repeat purchase.

Defer wall-mounted, structural, load-bearing, body-contact, toy, food-contact, water-handling, vehicle-critical, and complex configurable products. Their validation burden would hide whether the basic value proposition works.

## System-furniture opportunity

The 20 local system-furniture concepts are useful backlog evidence, not released products. Start with non-structural inserts only after measuring a real, identified product revision. Avoid product names that imply affiliation. Record the compatible manufacturer/system/revision as a factual fit statement after a documented physical test and legal review.

## Strategic questions still open

- Do buyers prefer entering measurements, selecting a known furniture system, or choosing standard variants?
- Is the strongest initial benefit “more usable space,” “less clutter,” “personal style,” or “confidence it will print”?
- What portion wants files versus finished objects?
- What error rate results from customer measurement?
- Will customers pay enough for documentation and support to fund testing?
- Which visual style best unifies utilitarian organizers and decorative storage?
