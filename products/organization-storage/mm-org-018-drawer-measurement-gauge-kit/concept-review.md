# Concept review

Selected concept: a coordinated flat kit with separate radius tiles, paired stepped-height cards, a monotonic clearance comb and an independent calibration frame.

This architecture keeps each reading physically simple and lets the user compare printed references with calipers before trusting them. Hole counts replace font-dependent numeric geometry on radius tiles; all exact values and units are repeated in the guide and worksheet. The two height cards measure taper indirectly through repeated width readings, which is more auditable than asking a user to visually match a small angle.

Rejected directions:

- Printed caliper or telescoping ruler: too much accumulated clearance and calibration risk for this phase.
- Single gear-shaped radius gauge: saturated competitor format and awkward in deep drawer corners.
- Printed 0.05–0.6 mm feeler leaves: below a robust two-line FDM feature for the reference process.
- Direct angle fan: matching a small wall draft by eye hides more uncertainty than paired width measurements.
- Automatic compensation factor: would silently mix printer error with user measurement error.

The SVG is a workflow diagram, not a manufacturing source. Decision: APPROVED under delegated authority.
