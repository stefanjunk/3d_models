# Concept review — PaletteGrid

Selected architecture: one open rail base, seven identical windowed dividers, and a two-part fit coupon.

Why it wins:

- It separates the collection-specific spacing from the long-lived base.
- Repeated 11.5 mm stations provide many layouts without brand presets.
- Identical dividers reduce source complexity, spares, and customer confusion.
- The open frame exposes covers and finger-access edges while cutting material.
- The fit key duplicates the real divider tongue, so the base is not the first tolerance print.

Rejected:

- Fixed comb: too much unused width and no adaptation to mixed thicknesses.
- Fully sliding dovetail dividers: unnecessary overconstraint and horizontal support risk.
- Tall single-body FDM rack: larger print, fewer replaceable parts, and poorer shipping/storage efficiency.
- Flexible clips against cosmetic cases: avoid sustained contact pressure and unsupported universal-fit claims.

The original interface is a two-rail, dual-foot vertical key with 0.5 mm total width clearance and 0.6 mm total tongue-length clearance at the nominal station.
